## Before

I built this alongside leading design for the company's largest product area. When I started, research was two people and a queue. A product manager asked for a study, a researcher ran it, and a report went into a folder. Most of the work was tactical usability testing, scheduled after a design was mostly decided. In the baseline year, 11% of delivered studies were strategic.

That model has a ceiling. Every request costs the same researcher hours, so the only way to do more is to hire more, and a service desk is hard to staff because nobody can point to what the last hire changed. The team was busy and mostly invisible.

## What I did

I grew the team from two researchers to six and embedded them with product areas instead of keeping them in a central pool. Embedding is easy to announce and hard to sustain, so most of my time went into the infrastructure underneath it.

**One front door.** Every request comes through intake and has to name the decision it informs. If nobody can name a decision, it isn't a study yet. That single question did more to change the portfolio than any planning session.

**Standard instruments, wired into the tickets.** Screeners, task scripts and survey items live as templates attached to the ticket type. Starting a study means starting from a tested instrument, not a blank document. Results also become comparable across studies, which matters later when you want to benchmark.

**A repository.** Findings are tagged and searchable, so the first question for any new request is what we already know. Sometimes the answer is enough.

**Unmoderated testing and a benchmark.** I added unmoderated testing so small evaluative questions didn't need a researcher's calendar, and set up a usability benchmarking program across five business lines so we had a baseline to measure improvement against.

**Two checkpoints, not constant supervision.** Designers and product managers run their own studies now. Quality holds because of two reviews: a plan review before anything reaches a participant, and a readout review before findings are shared. Researchers spend their judgment at the two points where it matters most and stay out of the way otherwise.

## What changed

Research output went from 45 studies in one year to 82 the following year, before that year was over. Strategic research went from 11% of delivered studies to 39%.

The second number is the one I care about. Doing more tactical work faster would have been easy to sell and would have kept research in the service desk. The shift in mix means research now shows up before decisions instead of after them.

The operating model also outlived its first year. It is written down in three successive annual strategies, each building on the last rather than replacing it. That is my real test for whether a function exists or is just a group of people with the same job title.
