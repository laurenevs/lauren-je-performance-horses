## Before

I led design for the company's largest product area and had built the research function alongside it. One pillar, one set of routines, teams that knew each other.

## What happened

Over the following period, my scope grew from that single pillar to the entire solutions portfolio. I absorbed the design team of a peer who was leaving and consolidated it with mine into a single organization with a shared operating rhythm. Later I restructured the organization from three areas into two. Across all of it, the function went through three reorganizations.

None of that is unusual. Most design leaders have lived through a reorg. What usually goes wrong is the period after the announcement, when the new chart exists on paper and the old routines are still running underneath it.

## What I did

**Rebuilt every routine the same day.** When the org went from three areas to two, every team ritual, review, channel and recurring meeting was rebuilt to the new shape on the day of the change. Nothing kept running on the old structure "for now." Dual-running is where accountability gets lost, because everyone can point to the old routine as the reason something slipped.

**Treated communications as part of the work.** How each change was communicated got as much design attention as the change itself. That approach was adopted as the model for the broader organization.

**Named the priorities and repeated them weekly.** I set five named portfolio priorities and made them a standing weekly artifact in every team and leadership channel. In a bigger org, most people only see their own channel. Repeating the same five priorities everywhere keeps cross-area work visible to people who would otherwise never see it.

**Gave the merged teams one operating rhythm.** Two teams with two ways of working aren't one organization yet. The consolidated org runs on a single shared rhythm.

## What changed

Design throughput on the teams I lead rose 43% year over year, from 2.1 to 3.0 projects per designer, during the same period as the reorganizations. Delivery cadence held through all three.

<figure>
  <img src="/ux/figures/throughput.svg" alt="Bar comparison of projects per designer. 2.1 in the prior year, 3.0 in the current year, a 43 percent increase." width="960" height="130" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>Projects per designer, year over year, measured by the <a href="/ux/work/instrumenting-design/">design scorecard</a>.</figcaption>
</figure>

I don't claim the reorganizations caused the increase. The point is that they didn't stop it, and the number was being measured the whole way through, so the claim can be checked.
