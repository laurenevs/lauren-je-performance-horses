## Before

Success metrics were inconsistent across product teams. When teams did define them, they ranged from a usage percentage picked out of the air, to an event count with no reason behind it, to "build a feature everyone loves." Follow-up was rare, so even good metrics never got checked.

## What I did

We read widely on existing metrics frameworks, argued about their tradeoffs and built one that fit how our teams worked. It's called **USER+**:

- **Usage.** Are customers using it? Feature adoption, active organizations, active users.
- **Satisfaction.** Are they happy using it? Product and customer satisfaction, with targeted follow-up.
- **Ease of use.** Is it easy to use? SUS or UMUX, heuristic evaluation scores, discoverability, ramp-up time.
- **Repeatability.** Are they coming back? Sessions per user over 30 days, product fit.
- **Plus a problem statement metric** for whatever the four above don't cover.

The framework came with a process. Before launch, the PM plans analytics with research support and the squad picks success metrics from the framework. At launch, research builds a dashboard. The following quarter, the team revisits the metrics and presents what happened in its quarterly update.

Rollout followed the same order as the argument: research leads, then UX leadership, then product leadership, then a pilot, then training.
