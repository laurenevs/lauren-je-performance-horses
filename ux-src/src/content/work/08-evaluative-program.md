## Before

App-wide surveys showed low ease-of-use scores, and follow-up research found that usability was a main driver of overall satisfaction. Yet no evaluative testing was happening, either on the live application or on new design work. There was no baseline to measure against.

## What I did

We started with measures: SUS and task pass/fail rates. The first goal was modest on purpose, two studies a month. We built the foundation that makes testing repeatable, including testing templates and analysis decks, so each study didn't start from scratch.

Once the cadence held, the goals moved: test earlier in the product lifecycle, and open testing up so designers and product teams could run evaluations themselves.

## What changed

We benchmarked the most important areas of the application and ran follow-up studies after improvements shipped, so we could show the change rather than claim it. Usability testing became standard practice across R&D, and we built up a repository of ready-made evaluative research. Product satisfaction scores rose over the same period.

## Where it shows up now

The usability benchmark across five business lines in [Building a research function](/ux/work/research-function/), and the two-checkpoint model that lets non-researchers run studies safely, both come from this.
