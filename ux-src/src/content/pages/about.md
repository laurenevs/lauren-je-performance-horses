## How I got here

I have an M.S. in Statistics and a B.S. in Applied Mathematics. I came into UX through research and quantitative methods, not visual design. My first UX job was running surveys and building a behavioral analytics program, and I still think about most problems as measurement problems first.

That background explains most of how I work. I want a definition before a dashboard, a baseline before a claim, and a median when the data has a long tail. When I say design got faster, I want to be able to show which number moved and where it came from.

Since then I've led UX teams at Workiva, GitLab and Optro. Most of that time has been in governance, risk and compliance software, with a stretch in developer operations at GitLab. These are complex products used by people whose work has real consequences, and that's where good UX changes the most.

Today I lead design and research together, in an organization where designers outnumber researchers. My focus is the agentic shift: deciding how AI should show up in the product so it changes how people actually do their work, and changing how my own team works along the way. The statistics background is how I lead, not what I do. It's why I tie the team's work to outcomes and can show which numbers moved.

## How I lead

I lead people first. I meet people where they are, figure out what each person needs, and make sure they have it so they can succeed. That looks different for everyone, because we're all human and we're not all the same.

I lead with compassion, and I don't micromanage. I like to inspire people. I want to work with fun people, have a good time, and do something really great together. The culture I build is fun, safe and focused on learning, and I don't tolerate toxic ones.

In my first 90 days, I do a listening tour, then set a direction based on what I heard.

A few things the case studies show, in case you're skimming:

- **I set direction.** The agentic experience framework guides design across five product lines.
- **I build the system, then step back.** Intake, review checkpoints and scorecards let a team do good work without me in every room.
- **I measure my own function.** If design or research can't show what it produced, it loses resourcing arguments to teams that can.
- **I change how I work before asking the team to.** I built my own AI tooling alongside backing the team's.
- **I'll tell you what didn't work.** One of the case studies is an investment that didn't get funded.

## What I look for in a team

The specifics depend on the role, but the core doesn't change. I look for people who will help build the culture I described above, on a strong team with high expectations of itself. I want a growth mindset, and short toes: people who don't take feedback personally, so we can disagree openly and keep moving.

## What I'm optimizing for next

I'm looking for a Head of UX role. I want to lead the function and build a large team. I'm most useful where there's real product complexity, a team that's ready to grow, and leadership that will act on evidence.
